GOALKICK - SHOT LIST
Read this in the field. Plain text so it opens anywhere.


BEFORE YOU START

1. Open the app, set the BALL SIZE. Note which ball you used.
2. Phone in landscape.
3. Pick a spot to the SIDE of the kick.


WHERE TO STAND

4. Stand so the ball flies ACROSS your screen - left to right, or
   right to left. Never toward you or away from you.
5. Pace out 10 to 12 paces from the ball. WRITE THE NUMBER DOWN.
6. Hold the phone level and at chest height. Do not tilt it up.


HOW TO FRAME IT

7. Put the ball about a quarter of the way in from one edge - the
   edge it is going to travel AWAY from.
8. Most of the screen should be the direction the ball is heading,
   not the space behind the kicker.
9. LOOK AT WHAT ELSE IS IN SHOT. Move every spare ball out of the
   picture, or well behind the one being kicked. A bag of balls on
   the touchline or a game on the next pitch will be found instead
   of yours. See WHY THREE OF THESE MATTER below.


EACH KICK

10. Ball sits STILL and in frame for at least 3 seconds first.
11. Start recording early. Stop recording late.
12. Hold the phone still. DO NOT PAN.
13. Ball must fly free. No net, no wall, nothing stopping it.
14. The ball must still be IN FRAME when it first hits the ground.
    Stand further back if it is not. See WHY FOUR OF THESE MATTER.


WHAT TO SHOOT

15. Five kicks at 1080p / 240.
16. Five kicks at 4K / 120. CHECK the setting between blocks - last
    time it came out three and eight instead of five and five.
17. For at least 3 of those kicks, PACE OUT WHERE THE BALL FIRST
    LANDS and write it down next to the clip time.
18. One extra kick with yourself deliberately at an angle, about
    30 degrees off to the side. This one is a test.


DO NOT

- Do not pan or follow the ball with the phone.
- Do not stand behind or in front of the kicker.
- Do not kick into a net.
- Do not tilt the phone up as the ball rises.
- Do not forget to set the ball size in the app.


WHY FOUR OF THESE MATTER

Item 14 is new and it is the one that got away on 22 August. The
software works out where the flight ended from the first bounce. On
seven of eleven clips the ball left the frame before it landed, and
those clips can never be checked against a paced distance - the
measurement is only half a flight. Standing further back costs a
little resolution on the ball and buys the whole trajectory.

Item 9 cost a whole session. On 22 August the pitch had a bag of
spare balls on the touchline and another game two pitches over.
The software found one of those instead of the ball being kicked
in nine clips out of eleven, and the tracks looked perfect while
measuring a ball 25 metres away. It picks the NEAREST ball now,
which is right as long as nothing rolls closer than yours.

Item 17 is the most valuable thing here. The app works out carry
distance from physics, and until 22 August nothing had ever
checked that number against reality. Pacing out where the ball
actually lands is the only way to find out whether the drag model
is any good. Paces are fine - counting the same way every time
matters more than accuracy.

Item 18 is a control. The app should flag that clip as off-square
and its gravity check should get worse in a predictable way. If it
does not, the warning is broken and we need to know.


GETTING THE CLIPS OFF THE PHONE

Use the cable. It is the only route that behaves predictably:

  Finder -> iPhone under Locations -> Files tab -> GoalKick -> Clips
  -> drag the .mov files out.

DO NOT rely on AirDrop for this. iOS decides for itself where an
AirDropped video goes and does not ask - it usually drops it into
Photos. Photos may retime high frame rate video, and if it does,
every speed the app calculates comes out eight times too slow with
nothing on screen to tell you.

If a clip does end up somewhere odd, the app's clip browser has an
Import button that can fetch it back out of Files.
