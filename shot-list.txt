GOALKICK - SHOT LIST
Read this in the field. Plain text so it opens anywhere.


TWO THINGS THAT STOP IT WORKING ENTIRELY - read this first

Everything else on this list makes the answer better or worse.
These two decide whether there is an answer at all. Measured 24
August. The software does not warn you about either one yet, so
they are on you.


LIGHT. Shoot in bright, direct daylight.

A fast ball smears across the picture during the moment the shutter
is open. Past about a third of the ball's width of smear, the
software stops finding the ball completely. Not "less accurately" -
it does not find it.

  Bright sun          fine
  Overcast            marginal, and worse the harder the kick
  Evening, dusk       no
  Under floodlights   no
  Indoors             no

Two things that will catch you out:

- A HIGHER FRAME RATE DOES NOT FIX THIS. 240 does not rescue a
  clip that 120 could not manage. In dim light both are far past
  the point where the ball is lost.
- HARD KICKS NEED MORE LIGHT THAN SOFT ONES. A gentle 13 m/s knock
  survives conditions that a real 30 m/s goal kick will not. Do not
  judge the light by how a warm-up clip looked.

If the phone's picture looks at all dim or muddy on screen, stop.
A clip shot in poor light is not a worse measurement, it is nothing
at all, and you will not find out until you are back at the Mac.


DISTANCE. There is a hard limit, and it depends on the format.

The software loses the ball once it is smaller than about 20 pixels
across. That puts a ceiling on how far away the ball can ever get:

  4K / 120       about 26 m   (roughly 28-30 paces)
  1080p / 240    about 13 m   (roughly 14 paces)

1080p has HALF the reach of 4K. That is not a small difference and
it is the single most useful thing on this page.

Remember the ball keeps getting further away after it is kicked.
Standing at 10 paces is fine; the ball flying out to 25 m is not,
if you are shooting 1080p. See WHY THIS FIGHTS ITEM 14 below.


BEFORE YOU START

1. Open the app, set the BALL SIZE. Note which ball you used.
2. Phone in landscape.
3. Pick a spot to the SIDE of the kick.


FIRST, THE CALIBRATION SHOT - takes ten minutes, no kicking

Bring a tape measure or measuring line that reaches 10 metres, the
ball you normally film with, inflated the way you normally inflate
it, something to rest the phone on, and something to write on.

A. Put the ball on flat ground in bright daylight, with no other
   footballs anywhere in the picture.
B. Set the phone 5 to 10 METRES from the ball - closer is more
   precise, and past about 13 m the software cannot see the ball at
   1080p at all.
C. Rest the phone on something solid - a wall, a bag, a fence post,
   a tripod. It has to sit completely still for two minutes.
D. Hold it level and in landscape, with the ball roughly centred.
E. MEASURE, do not pace, from the PHONE'S LENS to the CENTRE of the
   ball. Not from your feet. Not to the near edge.
F. Pull the tape tight and straight, read it to the nearest
   centimetre, and write it down in metres straight away.
G. Set the ball size in the app.
H. Film the ball sitting still for 10 seconds at 1080p / 240, with
   nobody in the frame.
I. Switch to 4K / 120 WITHOUT touching, nudging or repositioning
   the phone, and film the same ball in the same spot for 10
   seconds.
J. Write down the measured distance, both clip times, and which
   one is which format.

WHAT RUINS THIS SHOT

- Moving or bumping the phone between the two clips. The whole
  method depends on both clips sharing one viewpoint.
- Moving the ball between the two clips, for the same reason.
- Pacing the distance instead of measuring it. That is exactly
  what left this question open in the first place.
- Measuring to the near edge of the ball instead of its centre.
  At 5 m that is a 2% error, which is the same size as the thing
  being investigated.
- Anyone walking through the shot, or a second ball in frame.

Every one of those produces footage that looks perfectly fine and
is quietly useless, so get them right on the day.

This settles the camera's focal length, which every distance the app
computes is built on. The 4K value is confirmed. The 1080p value is
5% out and nobody knows whether that is the lens or a sloppy pace -
this shot answers it. Do it once and it never needs doing again.

It works even if the ball is not exactly the size it should be, and
even if the tape is slightly off, because both clips share one ball
at one distance and what matters is the two answers COMPARED. That
is why the ball itself does not need measuring.


WHERE TO STAND

4. Stand so the ball flies ACROSS your screen - left to right, or
   right to left. Never toward you or away from you.
5. Pace out 10 to 12 paces from the ball. WRITE THE NUMBER DOWN.
6. Hold the phone level and at chest height. Do not tilt it up.


HOW TO FRAME IT

7. Put the ball about a quarter of the way in from one edge - the
   edge it is going to travel AWAY from.
8. Most of the screen should be the direction the ball is heading,
   not the space behind the kicker.
9. LOOK AT WHAT ELSE IS IN SHOT. Move every spare ball out of the
   picture, or well behind the one being kicked. A bag of balls on
   the touchline or a game on the next pitch will be found instead
   of yours. See WHY THREE OF THESE MATTER below.


EACH KICK

10. Ball sits STILL and in frame for at least 3 seconds first.
11. Start recording early. Stop recording late.
12. Hold the phone still. DO NOT PAN.
13. Ball must fly free. No net, no wall, nothing stopping it.
14. The ball must still be IN FRAME when it first hits the ground.
    Stand further back if it is not - but SHOOT THESE IN 4K / 120.
    1080p runs out of reach at about 13 m and the ball vanishes
    mid-flight. See WHY THIS FIGHTS ITEM 14.


WHAT TO SHOOT

15. Five kicks at 1080p / 240.
16. Five kicks at 4K / 120. CHECK the setting between blocks - last
    time it came out three and eight instead of five and five.
17. For at least 3 of those kicks, PACE OUT WHERE THE BALL FIRST
    LANDS and write it down next to the clip time.
18. One extra kick with yourself deliberately at an angle, about
    30 degrees off to the side. This one is a test.


DO NOT

- Do not shoot in poor light. Nothing else on this list matters
  if the ball cannot be found.
- Do not shoot 1080p for a kick whose landing you need in frame.
- Do not pan or follow the ball with the phone.
- Do not stand behind or in front of the kicker.
- Do not kick into a net.
- Do not tilt the phone up as the ball rises.
- Do not forget to set the ball size in the app.


WHY FOUR OF THESE MATTER

Item 14 is new and it is the one that got away on 22 August. The
software works out where the flight ended from the first bounce. On
seven of eleven clips the ball left the frame before it landed, and
those clips can never be checked against a paced distance - the
measurement is only half a flight. Standing further back costs a
little resolution on the ball and buys the whole trajectory.

Item 9 cost a whole session. On 22 August the pitch had a bag of
spare balls on the touchline and another game two pitches over.
The software found one of those instead of the ball being kicked
in nine clips out of eleven, and the tracks looked perfect while
measuring a ball 25 metres away. It picks the NEAREST ball now,
which is right as long as nothing rolls closer than yours.

Item 17 is the most valuable thing here. The app works out carry
distance from physics, and until 22 August nothing had ever
checked that number against reality. Pacing out where the ball
actually lands is the only way to find out whether the drag model
is any good. Paces are fine - counting the same way every time
matters more than accuracy.

Item 18 is a control. The app should flag that clip as off-square
and its gravity check should get worse in a predictable way. If it
does not, the warning is broken and we need to know.


WHY THIS FIGHTS ITEM 14

Item 14 tells you to stand further back so the landing is in shot.
The distance limit tells you not to go too far back or the ball
disappears. Both are true and they pull against each other, so
here is how to settle it in the field.

  Shooting 4K / 120     you have room. The ceiling is about 26 m
                        and a normal kick lands well inside it.
                        Stand back as far as you need.

  Shooting 1080p / 240  you do not have room. The ceiling is about
                        13 m. A real goal kick will be past that
                        while it is still in the air, and the track
                        will just stop.

So: ANY CLIP THAT NEEDS THE LANDING IN SHOT SHOULD BE 4K / 120.
Shoot 1080p for kicks where you only care about how hard it was
struck, which is most of them in normal use - the app works out
carry and height from the launch alone and never needs to see the
ball land.

This also explains something from 22 August. Three of the eleven
clips were 1080p, and those three were the troublesome ones: one
failed outright, one came out suspect, and the only clean one was
the shortest kick of the day, which never got far enough away to
be lost. That may be coincidence with three clips. It matches the
measured limit exactly.


GETTING THE CLIPS OFF THE PHONE

Use the cable. It is the only route that behaves predictably:

  Finder -> iPhone under Locations -> Files tab -> GoalKick -> Clips
  -> drag the .mov files out.

DO NOT rely on AirDrop for this. iOS decides for itself where an
AirDropped video goes and does not ask - it usually drops it into
Photos. Photos may retime high frame rate video, and if it does,
every speed the app calculates comes out eight times too slow with
nothing on screen to tell you.

If a clip does end up somewhere odd, the app's clip browser has an
Import button that can fetch it back out of Files.
